# Master SRS traceability checklist

## Authority, scope and interpretation

Authoritative source: [UrbanTransit IQ — Data Science Intelligence Arena SRS, Version 1.0](documentation/UrbanTransit%20IQ-Data%20Science%20Intelligence%20Arena_SRS.pdf). Reviewed 2026-09-24. References use **physical PDF pages 1–54**, including cover and contents, rather than the table-of-contents offsets. Requirement IDs below are locally assigned traceability IDs; the SRS has section, step and Roman-numeral identifiers, not these IDs.

This is requirements analysis only. Planned implementations, technologies, evidence and tests are proposals for later work, not additional competition requirements or implementation claims. No code, datasets, installations, system configuration, or commits are part of this update. The reference website and sample architecture are illustrative; neither introduces extra requirements.

**Authority hierarchy:** (1) the official SRS PDF is the sole authoritative competition source; (2) this checklist is derived traceability/control documentation; (3) the dataset architecture, data dictionary and generation plan are implementation-design documents. If a derived document conflicts with the PDF, the PDF wins.

Classification rules:

- **Mandatory — MUST**: explicit must/required wording or a mandatory enclosing clause.
- **Mandatory capability — §1.7 blanket MUST; local SHOULD retained**: §1.7 explicitly says it is a must to implement functional and non-functional requirements. This makes §1.6/§1.7 capability coverage required, while local targets, applicability conditions and alternatives remain visible. Detailed Step-only SHOULD wording is not silently promoted; see ambiguity register.
- **Expected — SHOULD**: expected detail without an independent MUST. This is not the same as a MAY example.
- **Optional/example — MAY, “such as”, “possible”, “examples”, “recommended”**: individual examples are not mandatory. A surrounding mandatory capability remains required. Proposed coverage of an example does not change its normative status.
- **Alternative — OR**: select suitable alternatives; do not implement all choices merely because they appear in a list. Explicit obligations elsewhere still apply, especially Step 2 storage and the independent dual pipelines.
- **NOT STARTED**: no actual implementation/evidence verified. Existing directories, README and dependency placeholders do not satisfy implementation. Only the Development Log and AI declaration are marked PARTIAL because foundation text exists; neither is complete. No row is DONE.

Each matrix row is one tracked requirement/checklist item. Some source requirements are compound; their complete bullets remain in the source-text cell. Functional summaries and detailed steps overlap intentionally so neither source occurrence disappears. Deliverable content is split into individual checks. Totals count tracked rows, **not unique mandatory obligations**. Example rows are included in the total, not asserted to be mandatory. The final artifact checklist and risk register cross-reference existing rows and are not counted again.

## CRITICAL DISQUALIFICATION / INTEGRITY RISKS

Only SRS-supported risks are listed. The SRS explicitly attaches potential disqualification to item 1 below and potential reduced/zero module marks to item 2. It does **not** state that every other breach automatically disqualifies a team.

1. **Possible disqualification:** hard-coded predictions, fabricated accuracy, manually created dashboard values, or hidden decision APIs (§1.8(11), INT-11).
2. **Possible reduced or zero marks for the relevant module:** failure to explain submitted code (§1.8(15), INT-15). Evaluators may request explanations of transformations, queries, functions, features, models, forecasts and recommendations (INT-03) and pipeline disagreement (INT-10).
3. **Independent-pipeline breach:** copying Spark predictions into Python, or failing to demonstrate independently generated outputs (§1.8(8–9); STEP-42/43; INT-08/09).
4. **Prohibited external generative-AI output generation:** final demand/delay predictions, classifications and recommendations may not come from external generative-AI APIs; final analytics and forecasts must also come from the team's own logic (§1.8(14); §1.10(16); INT-14; DEL-AI-OUTPUT).
5. **Undeclared or unverified AI assistance:** all tools must appear in AI_USAGE.md; directly submitting AI-generated code/content without independent review, modification as required, testing and understanding violates the SRS (§1.8(12–13); §1.10(16); INT-12/13; DEL-16).
6. **Insufficient original dataset work:** downloading a ready-made dataset without significant transformation, integration and augmentation does not satisfy the requirement; own large interconnected realistic data and generation scripts are required (§1.2 Hint; DATA-01). Dataset minima remain mandatory (DATA-MIN-01–09).
7. **Missing competition participation evidence:** meaningful GitHub commits across all five days, every member's contribution, and the required Development Log must be demonstrated (§1.8(1–3); INT-01–03).
8. **Failure of live evaluation readiness:** teams must complete surprise modifications and diagnose/fix introduced defects; hidden transport data will be supplied (§1.8(4–7); INT-04–07). Listed changes and hidden-data conditions are possibilities, not an exhaustive contract.
9. **Invalid model evaluation:** missing train/validation/test separation, future-data leakage, failure to beat a documented forecasting baseline, or comparison on fewer than 100 unseen cases breaches explicit evaluation requirements (§1.2 Hint; Steps 23/24/44; DATA-04/05; STEP-24/44).
10. **Copying the reference application:** the SRS expressly requires an original application and prohibits copying/reproduction (§1.2 p.7; CORE-03).

## Ambiguities and interpretation decisions requiring confirmation

1. **SHOULD versus blanket MUST:** §1.6/§1.7 repeatedly say “should”; §1.7 then explicitly requires both entire sections. Required capability coverage is recorded, but targets retain their source wording and conditions. Confirm whether evaluators treat all detailed Step-only SHOULD fields as acceptance gates. No such promotion is assumed here.
2. **Step 12 heading:** “Real-Time Sound Monitoring” is followed entirely by overcrowding requirements. Preserve the heading; plan overcrowding analysis. No microphone/audio requirement is invented.
3. **Anomaly wording:** §1.6(xlvi) says “Normal passenger, delay, route, stop, and trip behavior should be detected”; Step 39 says unusual behavior. Both are preserved. Plan anomaly detection and normal controls; request correction of “Normal” rather than silently rewriting it.
4. **MAY capability versus mandatory summary:** delay severity classification and passenger segmentation are optional in Steps 20/40 but appear in §1.6. Plan those capabilities under the blanket MUST; exact severity labels, segmentation factors and example segment names remain choices.
5. **Connected tables and realistic conditions:** Hint uses “must ... such as”. The requirement for connected tables and difficult realistic data is mandatory; exact table names and each illustrative case are not separately declared MUST. Plan all named logical entities and example cases for coverage, but confirm if evaluators expect the entire illustrative lists. Step 1 expected content and functional data-management capabilities still apply.
6. **Dataset counting:** no precise grain is defined for “trip-level passenger records”, overlap with ticketing/movement records, historical-month boundary conventions, or “multiple service calendars and schedules”. Preserve exact minima; do not invent a minimum trip count, require both ticketing AND movement counts, add the two record minima together, or replace 12 months with a guessed day count. The project uses distinct `Passenger_Journeys` as its canonical movement view, reports the ticket/journey overlap and never adds them. Verify the evaluator's definition and counting treatment before final generation.
7. **Storage wording:** Step 2 requires demonstration of HDFS, CSV, JSON and Parquet (relational OR NoSQL where required); §1.9.2 lists storage alternatives and §1.6 says Hadoop-compatible storage. Plan the explicit Step 2 demonstrations and at least one large analytical Parquet dataset. An alternative listing does not waive those obligations. “Large” for that Parquet dataset is not numerically defined.
8. **Model counts:** Step 19 requires at least three suitable delay models; Step 41 requires at least three suitable Spark algorithms for applicable predictive tasks; DEL-05 repeats three suitable models. Do not assume every task needs three models or that Python has an unstated three-model minimum. Confirm permitted overlap between delay-model and Spark-model evaluations.
9. **Model metrics:** “at least 85% test accuracy or macro F1-score of at least 0.80 where appropriate” is an alternative classification target, not two mandatory metrics for regression. Forecast R² is conditional; MAPE needs a documented zero-demand policy. DEL-06 lists classification and forecast metrics without applicability qualifiers; document applicability and confirm how non-classification submissions should present them. Baseline type and minimum improvement margin are unspecified.
10. **Operational thresholds:** peak periods must be demand-derived; occupancy categories, overcrowding persistence, underutilization, anomaly cutoffs, delay thresholds, reliability weights, route-score weights, forecast horizon and high-risk probability cutoff are not numerically prescribed. Define and justify configurable choices later. The R12 recommendation example's 94%, 18 events, 21%, 17 minutes and 08:00–09:00 are illustrative, not universal thresholds.
11. **Performance/evaluation conditions:** “efficiently”, dashboard timing workload/concurrency, ten-million-record scaling verification, “normal conditions”, and the 99% uptime measurement window are unspecified. Hardware applicability to local versus hosted deployment is also unclear. Record test conditions and seek clarification; do not invent mandatory latency for raw processing or hardware purchase requirements.
12. **Deployment architecture:** deployment and a URL are mandatory; hosting alternatives are listed, but the SRS does not say every Hadoop/Spark component must run on the same public host or require live commercial integrations. Plan the deployable architecture later and demonstrate real processing provenance.
13. **Privacy/security:** applicable data protection regulations are not named, and no specific encryption, anonymization or retention mechanism is prescribed. Assess applicability and document chosen protections; do not mislabel proposed controls as quoted SRS obligations. Evaluator/administrator credentials are requested without credential-distribution details.
14. **Git timing and membership:** meaningful commits are required across all five competition days, but dates/timezone and member roster are not supplied by this PDF. Confirm the actual competition calendar and attribution process; no commits are authorized now.
15. **Source tree and exports:** §1.10(2) uses SHOULD for its file/directory layout; complete source and many artifacts are mandatory elsewhere. A literal directory layout is expected, not independently promoted to MUST. Downloadable report/export formats are unspecified; no PDF/CSV export mandate is invented.
16. **Sample architecture/reference:** the diagram is labelled sample and the reference site is optional inspiration. Neither mandates an extra component or allows copying. No external website was treated as a requirements authority.
